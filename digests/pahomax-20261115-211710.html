2Leaving pieces en prise, queen and knight especially

23 hung-piece tags, plus several opening blunders where the loss was a piece captured for free

You often move a piece, or a neighboring piece, and forget that something is now attackable. In the Nov 9 blitz game against a 323, 4... Qh4+ was met by 5. Nxh4, and the queen was lost. In the Nov 9 blitz game against a 301 you left a knight on e4 attacked: 6... Be7 allowed 7. Qxe4, and 7... Bxg5 repeated it with the same capture on e4. In the Nov 9 blitz game against a 263, 19... Qh5+ walked into 20. Qxh5 from a +10 position, when 19... Rf8 was the best move.

How to fix: After choosing a move, and before playing it, ask: what does my opponent's best reply capture? Then check every piece of yours that is attacked and not defended, including pieces you did not move. Before giving a check, check that the checking piece is safe.

  • Blunder check: before every move, scan for any of your pieces that are attacked (loose pieces drop off).
  • Do 'hanging piece' or 'loose piece' puzzles for 10 minutes before playing.
  • After the opponent's move, always ask 'what does that move attack?' before you think about your own plan.
  • Lichess puzzles: hanging piece
  • Chess.com puzzles

3Allowing mate threats and back-rank problems

5 allowed-mate tags, and 8 of your 20 losses ended in checkmate

When you are attacking or ahead, you sometimes forget your own king. In the Nov 15 blitz game against a 332, 27... Na4 let White play 28. Qxg7# from a +9.06 position, when 27... Rxb2 was winning. In the Nov 8 blitz game against a 262, 14. Ba4 allowed 14... Ne3+ 15. Kh2 Qg2#, when 14. fxg4 was best. In the Nov 10 blitz game against a 382, 30... Rxb4 allowed 31. Rc8# on the back rank with only 12.7 seconds on the clock. In the Nov 8 blitz game against a 297, 15... Ned7 allowed 16. Qxc7#.

How to fix: Add one question to every move: can my opponent give check, and where does the king go? Look for a back-rank escape square (luft) before you move a rook away from the first rank. Check opposing queen and rook lines to your king and your queenside squares like c7.

  • Play 'Checks first' training: every move, list all opposing checks after your intended move.
  • Solve mate-in-1 and mate-in-2 puzzles for the defender: find the square the mate threat uses.
  • Make luft (h3 or h6) early in endgames with rooks to avoid back-rank problems.
  • Lichess puzzles: exposed king
  • Lichess puzzles: kingside attack

Training plan

  1. Daily (15 min): tactics puzzles, solved slowly. Before each answer, list every check, capture and threat for both sides.
  2. Before every move in games: do the 3-step check: (1) what does their last move attack? (2) is any of my pieces loose? (3) can they check me? Use it in all blitz games.
  3. Weekly: review your 5 biggest blunders and note whether they were a missed tactic, a hung piece, or a mate against you.
  4. Mate-in-1 and mate-in-2 puzzles for 10 minutes, three times per week, to stop missing forced mates when you are winning.
  5. Time: play some 10-minute games instead of only 3+0 blitz, and keep at least 30 seconds for moves in won positions: several errors came at 12-17 seconds.
  6. Opening: pick one setup for White and one for Black, avoid early queen sorties, and learn the key pawn breaks (e5, c5) in each.
